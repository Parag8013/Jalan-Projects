Save each as the filename above its prompt, into `C:\JalanProjects\media\`.

---

## What these clips are for

They carry **mood, material and camera movement**. They do not carry information.

Every precise element — plot boundaries, dimension lines, counting figures, the acre comparator,
district maps, spec callouts — is **built in code** and composited on top. AI video cannot draw a
crisp line or a legible number, and code can, at any resolution, changeable in seconds.

So don't try to get Flow to produce diagrams, labels or measurements. Ask it only for a beautiful
physical model, moving.

clips will otherwise arrive as eight different films, and no grade can rescue that.

> Miniature architectural scale model photographed on a tabletop with a macro lens. Matte handmade
> materials: plaster, chipboard, unfinished MDF, fine sand. No gloss, no chrome, no glass, no neon.
> Soft overhead studio softbox lighting with gentle contact shadows. Muted desaturated palette of

## Before you send a clip

| Check | Fixable? |
|---|---|
| Camera moves one direction start to finish, never reverses | **No — regenerate** |
| Speed stays constant, no slowing at the end | **No — regenerate** |
| No cuts anywhere | **No — regenerate** |
| Materials look matte — no gloss, chrome or glass | **No — regenerate** |
| Lighting is soft and even, matching the other clips | **No — regenerate** |
| No text, numbers or signage in frame | **No — regenerate** |
| Objects morph, melt or warp during the move | **No — regenerate** |
| Colour too saturated or too bright | Yes — send it, graded in the build |
| Depth of field too strong or too weak | Yes — send it |

The three that matter most: **camera move**, **matte materials**, **soft even lighting**. A glossy or
neon-lit clip will not sit with the others no matter what I do to it.

---

## If a scene proves hard to generate

Clips 3 and 5 involve objects moving and assembling, which is the hardest thing to ask of a video
model. If they come out melting or jittering:

**Send the static version instead** — the same model, same lighting, camera move only, no assembly.
I can animate the subdivision and the frame assembly in code over a still or slow-moving plate. That
route is more work for me but produces a cleaner result, so it is a perfectly good outcome, not a
failure.

Do not spend a dozen credits fighting a clip. Two or three attempts, then fall back.
